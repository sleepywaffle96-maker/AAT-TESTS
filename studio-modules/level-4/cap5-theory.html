Unit: Personal Tax - Chapter 5 Theory

Lesson 5.1: Income Tax Computation Framework & Personal Allowance

Under the UK personal tax framework, individuals are liable to pay Income Tax on their total taxable income generated across a specific tax year. Income must be structured into three distinct legal categories, processed in a strict sequential order: Non-Savings Income (employment, trading, and property income), Savings Income (bank interest received), and Dividend Income.

The tax computation pipeline follows precise statutory tracking mechanisms:

  • Personal Allowance (PA): Individuals receive a tax-free threshold allowance (standardly £12,570) deducted from net income to establish the Taxable Income baseline.
  • The Personal Allowance Taper Reduction: If an individual's Adjusted Net Income crosses the statutory threshold of £100,000, the Personal Allowance is progressively reduced by £1 for every £2 of income above that mark, completely vanishing once income hits £125,140.
  • Tax Bands Allocation: Taxable income is loaded across progressive tax bands: the Basic Rate band, the Higher Rate band, and the Additional Rate band, applying matching percentage escalations to each income schedule.

Lesson 5.2: Employment Income Benefits (P11D) & National Insurance Classes

When individuals operate as employees, their taxable income extends beyond standard payroll salary. Employers must declare non-cash awards and perks under the statutory P11D Benefit-in-Kind (BIK) framework. Advanced technicians must calculate the exact cash equivalent value of these benefits:

  • Company Cars Benefit: Calculated by multiplying the vehicle's official list price by a statutory percentage derived directly from the car's carbon emissions (CO2 g/km) efficiency indicators.
  • Beneficial Loans: If an employer issues an internal loan to a worker at an interest rate below the official statutory rate, the interest savings gap is treated as taxable employment income.

National Insurance Contributions (NIC) Architecture

National Insurance represents an additional public levy collected to fund state welfare systems. It is split into separate regulatory classes based on the individual's employment status:

  • Class 1 (Primary & Secondary): Paid on employment earnings. Employees settle Class 1 Primary deductions via PAYE, while employers contribute Class 1 Secondary levies on identical worker payrolls.
  • Class 2 & Class 4: Enforced strictly on self-employed sole traders. Class 2 represents a small weekly flat-rate charge, whereas Class 4 is a profit-based percentage tracking net adjusted trading income fields.
"Calculating benefits and NIC requires absolute compliance with annual tax tables. Erroneous benefit evaluations or failure to apply correct threshold caps triggers severe administrative penalties from HMRC during personal tax audits."

Lesson 5.3: Individual Capital Gains Tax (CGT) & Inheritance Tax (IHT)

When an individual disposes of a personal asset (such as shares, land, or secondary residential property) and registers a capital profit, this transaction falls under the **Capital Gains Tax (CGT)** framework. Unlike corporate chargeable gains, individuals process these events completely separated from their regular income tax schedules.

The structural calculation of an individual capital gain requires applying allowances and behavioral tax rates:

INDIVIDUAL CAPITAL GAINS TAX FRAMEWORK:
1. Net Capital Gain = Disposal Proceeds - Acquisition Cost - Disallowed Transfer Expenditures
2. Taxable Capital Gain = Net Capital Gain - Annual Exempt Amount (AEA statutory exemption limit)
3. CGT Scaling Rates: Basic rate income taxpayers pay lower CGT percentages, while higher or additional rate earners face maximized scaling brackets, with residential properties attracting specific surcharges.

Inheritance Tax (IHT) Principles

Inheritance Tax is levied on the estate of a deceased person and on certain lifetime transfers. The core framework incorporates precise safety thresholds:

  • Nil Rate Band (NRB): The standard tax-free threshold baseline (standardly £325,000) below which an estate pays 0% IHT.
  • Lifetime Transfers: Gifts made to individuals are classified as Potentially Exempt Transfers (PETs), becoming entirely tax-free if the donor survives for 7 years after the gift event.

⚠️ Personal Tax Invisible Error Encyclopedia (AAT Core Exam Traps)

  • The Personal Allowance Taper Trap: A major calculation mistake is forgetting to taper the Personal Allowance when Adjusted Net Income passes £100,000. For every £2 of income above this limit, £1 of allowance is permanently lost, which dramatically shifts the tax band loading.
  • The Company Car List Price Illusion: When computing car benefits, exam candidates often mistake the actual purchase price paid by the company for the vehicle's list price. Under HMRC rules, you must **always use the manufacturer's official list price**, ignoring any internal corporate discounts.
  • The Dividend Tax-Free Band Allocation: Failing to remember that the dividend tax-free allowance still consumes space within the tax bands is a classic exam pitfall. Even though the dividend allowance has a 0% tax rate, it counts toward the basic rate band limit, pushing subsequent income into higher bands.

AAT LEVEL 4 RECAP

Chapter 6: External Auditing Quick Cheat Sheet

🎯 EXAU RISK & EVIDENCE MEMORY BLOCK:

• Audit Risk Equation: Inherent Risk × Control Risk × Detection Risk.
• Benchmarks: 0.5%-1% Revenue | 1%-2% Total Assets | 5%-10% Net Profit.
• Procedures Split: Vouching Backward (Existence) | Tracing Forward (Completeness).

1. Audit Risk Model & Materiality Baselines

  • The Risk Interaction Matrix: If Inherent Risk (IR) is high and Control Risk (CR) is high due to system gaps, the auditor must decrease Detection Risk (DR) by expanding sample sizes.
  • Materiality Parameters: Omissions are material if they alter stakeholder decisions. Performance Materiality acts as a lower buffer threshold to capture cumulative uncorrected balance errors.
  • Evidence reliability parameters: Third-party external certificates (bank confirmations) rank high; internal manager spreadsheets rank low in reliability audits.

2. Substantive Testing Directions & Audit Opinion Frameworks

  • Vouching Verification: Select transactions from ledger summaries and track them backward to source data to confirm Existence / Occurrence.
  • Tracing Verification: Select shop floor physical documentation records and track them forward into ledger totals to confirm Completeness.
  • Report Opinion Options: A clean report page receives an Unmodified opinion. If errors are material and widespread across data schedules, they are classified as **pervasive**, triggering an Adverse Opinion or a Disclaimer.
⚠️ ADVANCED EXAM TRAPS RECAP (INVISIBLE ERRORS):
  • The Absolute Assurance Illusion Trap: Stating that an external audit guarantees total absence of errors. Due to testing samples and human estimates, audits deliver strictly **Reasonable Assurance**.
  • The Audit Risk Relationship Flaw: Assuming that broken internal company controls allow a reduction in substantive tests. High Control Risk requires lower Detection Risk targets.
  • The Directional Inversion Oversight: Traced a ledger summary backward to test for Completeness. Testing for completeness requires moving forward from original source documents to ledger cells.
